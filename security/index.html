Security

Telephony is a target. Act like it.

Public SIP endpoints are scanned around the clock by fraud bots hunting for open PBXs and weak credentials. Sirius Telefonix is built on the assumption that the scanning never stops — so the platform filters hostile traffic at the edge and keeps the voice core off the internet entirely.

Six layers before a call gets in.

Every packet that arrives at the SIP edge runs a gauntlet. Fail any layer and the request is dropped silently — attackers learn nothing, and repeat offenders are banned without a human in the loop.

  1. 01

    Known-bad sources are dropped first

    A ban list is checked before anything else. Traffic from banned sources is discarded silently — no response, no information leaked back to the sender.

  2. 02

    Floods are detected and cut off

    Per-source rate analysis spots SIP floods and scanner bursts. Offenders are cut off and banned automatically.

  3. 03

    Geography is policy — and it fails closed

    A configurable geographic allowlist decides where calls may come from. If the policy data is unavailable or a lookup fails, the request is denied — when in doubt, nothing gets in.

  4. 04

    Identities must match your patterns

    Registration and call attempts are checked against expected identity patterns. Requests that don’t belong are dropped, and their source is banned.

  5. 05

    Concurrent call quotas per caller, tenant, and trunk

    Dialog tracking at the SIP edge enforces configurable concurrent-call ceilings per source IP, per tenant, and per outbound trunk — preventing a single runaway loop or compromised endpoint from exhausting capacity.

  6. 06

    Behavioral toll-fraud detection & dialog validation

    Outbound international prefixes are gated by policy, rapid short-call bursts and sequential destination scanning trigger automatic blocks, and in-dialog requests are validated sequentially.

The voice core never touches the internet.

Only two components face outward: the SIP edge for signaling and the media anchor for voice streams. The PBX, the control plane, and the databases are private to the cluster — unreachable from outside by design, with the web console served separately behind TLS.

Topology hiding strips internal addresses from everything that leaves the edge, and call identifiers are protected against dialog-correlation tricks — an outside observer learns nothing about how your cluster is laid out.

TLS-capable signaling, certificates on autopilot.

The SIP edge accepts UDP, TCP, and TLS signaling, with certificates issued and renewed automatically — no manual certificate ceremony. Clients that offer encrypted media (SRTP) have it terminated at the media anchor and bridged onward inside the private network.

The agent desk’s browser phone is a SIP endpoint like any other: its WebSocket signaling terminates at the SIP edge, behind the same policy as every phone, and its encrypted browser media (DTLS-SRTP) is terminated at the media anchor. The PBX opens no WebRTC listener of its own.

The same automation covers the web front ends: the admin console and the agent desk are served over HTTPS with automatically managed certificates.

Your calls are your business. Literally.

Storage you control

Recordings, voicemail, prompts, and reports live in S3-compatible object storage on your infrastructure — with automated 90-day retention policies to keep storage bounded.

Secrets handled properly

Deployment credentials are generated locally at install time, and database passwords rotate cluster-wide with a single command.

Least-privilege data layer

Two-tier database accounts separate administration from the application’s runtime access.

Passkeys, 2FA, and Enterprise SSO

Sign in with WebAuthn/FIDO2 passkeys, TOTP two-factor authentication with backup recovery codes, or Enterprise SSO across OIDC, SAML 2.0, and LDAP.

Maker-Checker dual control

Sensitive administrative actions can require secondary review and approval before taking effect, preventing unilateral changes to critical configuration.

Audit trail & SIEM streaming

Configuration changes are versioned in an Envers audit trail, API access is scoped by role and tenant, and security events stream in real-time CEF format to your SIEM.
